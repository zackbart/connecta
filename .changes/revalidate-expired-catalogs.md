A call whose admission depends on a write restriction the host's approval does not cover now lists its connector's catalog again before admission when the copy its request retained has passed its TTL (#1414). That covers every call from `execute_read_code` and from `execute_code` on a read-only endpoint, and calls from writable `execute_code` or `call_destructive_tool` to a `readOnly` connector or to a tool a `readOnly: true` pool's `writes` does not grant. Previously a program admitted every call against the catalog listed at its first use, so a downstream that reclassified a tool from read to write mid-program, without sending `notifications/tools/list_changed`, could have that tool dispatched past the restriction. It is now refused (`destructive_tool_requires_approval` or `read_only_endpoint`) and nothing is sent. Calls within a catalog's TTL list nothing again, concurrent calls share one relisting, and a zero-TTL catalog is listed again before each later such call. Unrestricted writable programs, granted writes and `call_tool` keep the catalog their request retained.
